Eval IQ Privacy Policy

Effective date: October 24, 2026
Applies to: Eval IQ for iPhone, iPad, and Mac, published by David McDonald

The short version

Eval IQ doesn't collect any data. We have no servers, no accounts, no analytics, no advertising, and no tracking. Everything you enter stays on your iPhone, iPad, or Mac.

Information leaves your device only when you choose to send it: by sharing a PDF report, by exporting a passphrase-encrypted backup file, or by turning on iCloud and computer backups for your records (off by default).

What the app stores, and where

Eval IQ keeps what you enter in a private database inside the app, on your device. That includes educators, observation evidence and any photos you attach, scores, conference notes, and Professionalism ratings. Your name, your school, and your app preferences are saved in the app's settings on the same device.

  • We never receive any of it. The app doesn't connect to the internet, and it has no account to sign in to.
  • iOS encrypts it. The app's files use Apple's strongest file protection, so they're encrypted whenever your device is locked.
  • Photos: you can take a photo with the camera or pick one through Apple's photo picker. Eval IQ receives only the photos you take or choose, and it can't see the rest of your photo library. Photos taken in Eval IQ are not saved to your photo library.
  • Documents you upload: when you upload a PDF, Word document, or text, Eval IQ reads its text on your device. Scanned pages are read with Apple's on-device text recognition. The original file is kept only if you choose to keep it, and it's included in your encrypted backups.
  • Recordings: Eval IQ uses the microphone only while you're recording, after you tap Record. Recordings, and audio files you choose, are transcribed on your device by Apple's speech recognition. The recording is kept on your device with its transcript so you can play it back; before adding it, you can turn off "Keep the recording" to keep only the text. A kept recording is stored like your other records, is included in encrypted backups you export, and is removed when you delete its evidence, the observation, or all data. The first time, your device may download Apple's speech model for your language. That download comes from Apple, and no audio is sent.
  • Faces in photos: before a photo is saved, Eval IQ finds faces in it on your device using Apple's Vision framework, so you can blur any student whose parent opted out of photos. Only the finished image is stored, and the unblurred original is never saved. Eval IQ doesn't recognize or identify anyone. It records only how many faces were found and blurred, and when you confirmed the opt-outs. You can turn classroom photos off in Settings.

Calendar (optional)

If you turn on Settings › Calendar › Add to my calendar, Eval IQ adds the observations and conferences you book to the calendar you choose, and moves or removes them when you reschedule. It also reads your other calendar events on your device, only to warn you about scheduling conflicts. Eval IQ never sends your calendar anywhere.

Events say only "Observation" or "Post-conference" unless you turn on teacher names. If your calendar syncs to a district, school, or cloud account, events Eval IQ adds sync there like any other event, so anyone with access to that calendar can see them. Turning the setting off lets you remove Eval IQ's events.

Teacher invites: when you tap Send invite, Eval IQ creates a calendar file and opens the share sheet. You choose whether and how to send it. Eval IQ never sends invites by itself.

iCloud and device backups

By default, your evaluation records are excluded from iCloud Backup and computer backups, so they exist only on this device.

You can change this in Settings › Privacy & security › Include in iCloud / device backups. If you turn it on, your records are included in the backups that Apple or your computer makes under your own account. We still never receive them. Only turn it on if your school or district allows evaluation records in personal backups.

Your app preferences, such as the name and school printed on reports, are included in normal device backups, like any app's settings. Eval IQ never syncs with iCloud.

On-device AI (Apple Intelligence)

On devices that support Apple Intelligence, with Apple Intelligence turned on, "Score with AI" reads the evidence you submitted, links it to the rubric indicators it supports, suggests a score for each indicator, and proposes an area of reinforcement and an area of refinement with a rationale and next steps. Eval IQ can also draft post-conference talking points and suggest questions for each part of the conference. It uses Apple's on-device foundation model, which runs on your device. The model reads text only: notes, transcripts, document text, and photo captions, not images or audio. Eval IQ doesn't send your evidence to us or to any other AI service.

Suggestions are drafts. You review, edit, or reject each one, and you decide every score. If Apple Intelligence isn't available, the AI features are hidden and the rest of the app works the same.

Face ID lock (optional)

You can require Face ID, Touch ID, or your device passcode to open Eval IQ: Settings › Privacy & security › Require Face ID or passcode. iOS performs the check and tells Eval IQ only whether it succeeded. Eval IQ never sees or stores your face data, fingerprint, or passcode. While the lock is on, Eval IQ also hides its contents in the app switcher.

When information leaves your device

This happens only when you choose to send it.

  1. PDF reports. When you export a report and tap Share, you pick where it goes, such as AirDrop, Mail, Files, or a printer. A report contains the details of that observation or rating, plus the evaluator name and school you entered in Settings. After you share it, the app or person you sent it to controls that copy.
  2. Encrypted backups. When you create a backup, Eval IQ encrypts all your records, including photos and kept recordings, with AES-256 using a passphrase you choose (8 or more characters). You decide where to save the file. We never see your passphrase, so we can't open your backup or recover a lost passphrase.
  3. iCloud and computer backups, if you turn them on. See "iCloud and device backups" above. These backups belong to your Apple account or your computer, not to us.

What we don't do

  • We don't have accounts or sign-in.
  • We don't use analytics, crash-reporting, or advertising code.
  • We don't track you across apps or websites.
  • We don't include any third-party code that collects data.
  • We don't sell or share data. We don't have any.

Information Apple may share with us

If you've chosen in iOS to share analytics with app developers (Settings › Privacy & Security › Analytics & Improvements › Share With App Developers), Apple may give us crash reports and overall usage statistics for Eval IQ. Apple collects and controls this information, not Eval IQ, and you can turn it off at any time.

Deleting your data

  • Delete all data: Settings › Delete all data. This permanently deletes every educator, observation, rating, evidence item, photo, and kept document, plus your settings, and returns Eval IQ to how it was when first installed. You confirm by typing DELETE, and with Face ID, Touch ID, or your passcode if App Lock is on.
  • Delete the app: this removes everything Eval IQ stored on the device. Reports or backups you saved somewhere else aren't affected.

We don't hold any of your data, so there's nothing for us to access, correct, or delete for you.

Children

Eval IQ is a professional tool for school evaluators and isn't directed to children.

Changes to this policy

If we change this policy, we'll update this page and the effective date above. If a future version of Eval IQ ever handles data differently, we'll update this policy before that version is released.
